What does a weather disruption mean?

Weather can reduce the number of aircraft an airport or route can handle. A weather disruption is a description of conditions, not a separate FAA program: weather can lead to a Ground Stop or Ground Delay Program. A METAR reports observed airport weather; it is not a flight-status report.

Arrivals, departures and your flight

For arrivals, weather at the destination or along the route can restrict traffic. For departures, local conditions or restrictions at the destination can hold a flight. For a single flight, neither an alert nor a METAR establishes a delay or cancellation; ask the airline.

What you can do now

  • Check the airline’s own status page through the airlines index.
  • Ask the airline for rebooking before paying for another ticket, meal or hotel. Ask what it will authorize in writing.
  • Keep receipts and the airline’s messages for any claim.

What is not known

This page cannot establish when a disruption ends, when your flight departs or arrives, or whether a connection works. A published program end time can change. Missing or stale data never means clear. Check directly with the airline.
