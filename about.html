About Air Travel Desk

What this is

Air Travel Desk is an independent project that shows US airport delays and ground stops from public data, with a live stream on Twitch and this website.

Every source and its terms are on the Data and sources page.

We are not affiliated with any airline, airport or government agency.

Our rules

  • Facts only. We say what the source reports, where, and when. We give no opinion, no blame and no prediction.
  • Official sources come first. Each item names its source and the time the source reported it.
  • Plain words. Numbers appear as the source gives them.
  • The same rules apply to every airport and every airline.
  • The order of items follows size, severity, flights affected and how recent the report is. Nothing else changes the order.
  • We say "reported" or "estimated" when a figure is not final, and we correct errors fast and say what changed.
  • The stream host may choose what to show and in what order. It does not add a fact that is not in the data.

How pages are made

The live status board, weather notes, radar image, aircraft map and travel numbers are updated automatically from public data by a script. The site tries to refresh about every 5 minutes and keeps the last good data if a source is late.

The wording in the I'm-stuck guide and the airport guide link lists are written by hand. The Ask before you pay section states only rules from U.S. DOT pages we read ourselves on 2026-11-14, and each rule shows its link and read date. On 2026-11-14 a script tested the airport links. Some airport sites block that test, so we could not verify every link by script, and the airport pages say so.

Nothing on this site is written by a reader or a paid party. There are no ads on this site yet.

The night-airfield pictures are generated images in a house style. They are decoration. They show a kind of airfield, never a real event, aircraft or airport, and some change with an airport's status or a step in the guide.

Where this site uses "what you may be owed" or "possibly owed" wording, it is plain-language help based on public U.S. Department of Transportation rules and airline customer service plans, not legal advice. Your airline decides every claim, voucher or reimbursement.

Where the data comes from

Every feed is public. The full table, with licences and the last time we checked each feed, is on the Data and sources page.

  • FAA and U.S. Department of Transportation. FAA NAS status gives airport delays, ground stops and closures. The DOT Airline Cancellation and Delay Dashboard gives the airline commitments table, shown on the airline pages.
  • Weather. The NOAA National Weather Service gives alerts and radar. aviationweather.gov gives airport weather reports, forecasts and SIGMETs. The NOAA Storm Prediction Center gives storm outlooks and the National Hurricane Center gives active storms.
  • Aircraft positions. adsb.lol gives the positions behind the map trails and traffic counts, under the ODbL licence.
  • Maps and places. OpenStreetMap gives hotels and car rental near each airport. Natural Earth gives the state outlines on the map.
  • Statistics. TSA checkpoint numbers and Bureau of Transportation Statistics on-time data give travel counts and monthly on-time rates.

Airport guide links come from the airports' own websites. Airport pages cover the 100 busiest U.S. airports. For the 70 after the first 30, the airport's website address comes from OurAirports (public domain) and that is the only link we list. Hotel and car rental lists appear on a page only when we have them.

Limits of the data

  • Delays shown here are what the FAA NAS status feed lists, not every individual flight delay.
  • Aircraft positions are a sample with a delay, not a real-time radar feed.
  • Weather reports, forecasts, radar and storm areas are a snapshot from the latest automated run.
  • Check with your airline and the airport before you travel.
  • This site is not for flight dispatch or navigation.
