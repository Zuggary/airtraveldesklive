Ask before you pay

Before you pay for a meal or a hotel, ask the airline if it will help. Ask first. Then decide.

Plain-language help, not legal advice. Each DOT rule below has a link to the U.S. DOT page and the date we read it. This section works without JavaScript.

Why ask first

Some airlines pay for a meal or a hotel when they cause the delay or cancellation. Some do not. It depends on the cause and on the airline's own plan.

The U.S. DOT says each airline has its own policies for delayed and cancelled passengers. If an airline has made a commitment, DOT can hold it to that. Source: U.S. DOT: Airline Cancellation and Delay Dashboard, read 2026-11-14.

DOT tells travelers to ask airline staff if they will pay for meals or a hotel room. It says some airlines offer these and others may not. Source: U.S. DOT: Airline Cancellation and Delay Dashboard, read 2026-11-14.

We found no DOT page that says every airline has to pay for meals or a hotel. The airline's own plan decides. Source: U.S. DOT: Airline Cancellation and Delay Dashboard, read 2026-11-14.

So ask before you pay. If you pay first, the airline may or may not pay you back.

What to say
  • At the counter: “My flight is [delayed / cancelled]. Does the airline offer a meal or a hotel for this?”
  • On the phone: “My flight is [delayed / cancelled]. What is the reason in your system, and is a meal or hotel voucher available?”
  • In chat: “Flight [FLIGHT] is [delayed / cancelled]. What is the reason? Can you give me a meal or hotel voucher, or tell me why not?”

Be polite and clear. Say what you need: a meal, a hotel, or a new flight.

DOT says airport customer service representatives may be able to arrange meals and hotel rooms for stranded passengers. Look for them first. Source: U.S. DOT: File a Consumer Complaint, read 2026-11-14.

What to ask for in writing
  • The reason for the delay or cancellation. Ask for the cause code if there is one.
  • The voucher, or a confirmation that they said yes or no.
  • The agent's name or ID number.
  • The time you spoke with them.

A text, an email, or a photo of a printed slip all work. After a phone call, write the details down at once and ask for a confirmation email.

Airline cause or weather?

The cause usually matters. Ask for it in writing.

Airline-caused delays and cancellations are what the dashboard commitments are for. DOT's examples are maintenance or crew problems, cabin cleaning, baggage loading and fueling. Source: U.S. DOT: Airline Cancellation and Delay Dashboard, read 2026-11-14.

Weather, air traffic control and security are not what the dashboard commitments are for. For these causes, help is usually up to the airline. Some airlines still help, so ask anyway. Source: U.S. DOT: Airline Cancellation and Delay Dashboard, read 2026-11-14.

On the dashboard, Yes means the airline has committed to it. No means it has not made that commitment, but the airline may still help at its own discretion. Source: U.S. DOT: Airline Cancellation and Delay Dashboard, read 2026-11-14.

If the agent says weather and you think the airline caused the problem, ask for the reason in writing.

The DOT rules that apply

Refund for a cancelled flight. If the airline cancels your flight, for any reason, and you choose not to travel or take a voucher or credit, DOT says you can get a refund of your ticket. Source: U.S. DOT: Refunds, read 2026-11-14. Source: 14 CFR 260.6, read 2026-11-14.

A big delay or schedule change counts too. DOT's example is arriving 3 hours or more later than planned on a U.S. domestic trip. If you fly the new flight anyway, DOT rules do not give you a refund. Source: U.S. DOT: Refunds, read 2026-11-14.

Under 14 CFR 260.6 and the DOT refund page, if an airline offers a voucher or credit instead of a refund, it has to tell you about your right to a refund. The voucher or credit has to last at least five years. Source: U.S. DOT: Refunds, read 2026-11-14. Source: 14 CFR 260.6, read 2026-11-14.

Rebooking. Airlines often offer a new flight. DOT's page says rebooking is not required by DOT. On the dashboard (last updated 2025-09-11), every airline in the tables has committed to rebook you on its own flights when it causes the problem. Source: U.S. DOT: Refunds, read 2026-11-14. Source: U.S. DOT: Airline Cancellation and Delay Dashboard, read 2026-11-14.

The dashboard. It shows what large U.S. airlines have committed to when they cause a delay or cancellation. DOT says airlines are required to keep the promises in their customer service plans. Source: U.S. DOT: Airline Cancellation and Delay Dashboard, read 2026-11-14.

Long waits on the ground in a plane. DOT says that on a flight in the U.S., the airline has to give you a chance to get off, or start moving to a place where you can, before 3 hours (domestic) or 4 hours (international). Exceptions are for safety, security or air traffic control reasons. DOT also says the airline has to give you a snack and water within 2 hours, unless the pilot decides safety or security does not allow it. Source: U.S. DOT: Tarmac Delays, read 2026-11-14.

Complaints. Try the airline first. DOT says the airline has to acknowledge your complaint within 30 days and answer in writing within 60 days. If that does not solve it, you may file a complaint with DOT. DOT does not investigate every complaint. Source: U.S. DOT: File a Consumer Complaint, read 2026-11-14.
